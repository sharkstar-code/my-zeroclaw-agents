Brian Barry Memorial Lecture: Chiara Cordelli on Political Philosophy

TL;DR An annual institutional marker of serious political philosophy, featuring Chiara Cordelli—a theorist of justice, responsibility, and the state—at LSE's most prestigious lecture platform.

Why it Matters

The Brian Barry Memorial Lecture is LSE's flagship annual event honoring a political philosopher of genuine significance. Barry himself was a theorist of justice and democratic theory who refused easy consensus; the memorial lecture series carries that intellectual DNA. Chiara Cordelli is a contemporary theorist working at the intersection of political philosophy, philosophy of law, and ethics—her work interrogates concepts like responsibility, state legitimacy, and what we actually owe each other in complex social arrangements.

The fact that LSE hosts this as a major public event signals that political philosophy remains contested intellectual terrain, not settled doctrine. The lecture will likely engage fundamental questions about how we structure collective life—not abstract theorizing, but philosophy with real political stakes.

Where and When

LSE Old Building, London
Wednesday, May 20, 2026 | 6:30 PM (18:30) onwards

Access & Logistics

  • Likely free and open to public
  • Seating on first-come, first-served basis
  • Arrive 15-20 minutes early
  • The LSE Old Building is architecturally significant—Victorian institutional power—which frames the event as part of a longer tradition of political thought
  • Expect an audience of academics, policy intellectuals, serious students, and politically-minded Londoners

Insider Note: This is institutional philosophy at its best—rigorous, public-facing, and genuinely concerned with how we should live together. If you're interested in contemporary political theory that actually engages with real-world complexity (not ideological purity), Cordelli's work is essential. The memorial lecture format suggests this will be a polished, major statement of her thinking.
